## About the source

[**28BYJ-48**](https://cookierobotics.com/042/) is a hands-on teardown and explanation published by **Cookie Robotics**. Its stated purpose is to understand the motor by disassembling it, recording physical observations, inferring how it works, and checking its response to different electrical pulse sequences. The article does not clearly identify an individual author or a publication date, so neither is assumed here.

## Three highlights

### 1. Anatomy: three systems in one small motor

The 28BYJ-48 contains a **gear train**, a **stator**, and a **permanent-magnet rotor**. Its output shaft is not driven directly: the rotor turns a sequence of gears housed above the electromagnetic assembly. The stator has two coils and claw-shaped metal pole pieces; the familiar five-wire connection is characteristic of the unipolar motor configuration described in the article.

![Cross-sectional illustration of the internal gear train, rotor, and stator.](https://cookierobotics.com/042/cross_section.png)

*Image/source: [Cookie Robotics — cross-section](https://cookierobotics.com/042/cross_section.png), from [28BYJ-48](https://cookierobotics.com/042/).*

### 2. Gear reduction: a small rotor produces a slow output

Instead of relying on a label, the author **counts gear teeth** and determines a **64:1 reduction** for the examined motor. For every full turn of the output shaft, the rotor turns approximately 64 times. The gearbox converts relatively rapid internal stepping into slower, finer output motion with greater available output torque, at the cost of speed and with mechanical losses.

The article cautions that similarly labelled motors may have **different gear ratios depending on the manufacturer**. Using this specimen's reduction, 32 full steps per rotor revolution correspond to approximately **2,048 full steps per output revolution**; half-step operation corresponds to approximately **4,096 half steps**. These are not universal calibration values for every 28BYJ-48.

*Source: [Cookie Robotics — Gear Train and Driving the Motor](https://cookierobotics.com/042/).*

### 3. Magnetism: motion by sequential alignment

The rotor is a **permanent magnet with eight alternating north–south pole pairs**. Current through the stator coils magnetizes their claw-like poles. Switching which windings are energized changes the magnetic alignment the rotor seeks, advancing it from one stable angular position to the next. This is why the 28BYJ-48 can be controlled as a **unipolar permanent-magnet, claw-pole (can-stack) stepper motor**.

## Relevance to kinetic mechanisms

This teardown makes it easier to think about the motor as a physical system rather than a black-box actuator: its internal magnetic steps, gear ratio, and drive sequence all affect the movement visible at the output. For slow kinetic sculptures, the key design questions are the needed angular resolution, holding torque, speed, and whether the coils should remain energized while the object is stationary.

A particularly useful observation in the article is that energizing two coils increases the rotor's resistance to displacement but also increases current consumption and heating. The author additionally provides a **[reconstructed STEP model](https://cookierobotics.com/042/)** (linked near the beginning of the article), potentially useful when designing surrounding mechanical parts.
