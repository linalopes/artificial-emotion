## A nearby material resource

[HRH Fishing Hebeisen](https://hebeisen.ch/) is a fishing-supply store at **Zürcherstrasse 49, 8620 Wetzikon**, close enough to the studio that it can function as a practical extension of the material research.

What makes the shop useful is not only that I can buy fishing equipment there. Fishing already contains a highly developed technical vocabulary around **thin lines, tension, rotation, knots, weights, guides, pulleys, connectors and controlled movement**.

Many of these objects are small, inexpensive and designed to solve exactly the kinds of mechanical problems that appear repeatedly in kinetic work.

Being able to walk to the shop, look at the hardware directly, ask questions and return to the studio with a possible solution makes it different from simply ordering components online.

## Braided fishing line

For the current kinetic experiments I bought **0.16 mm braided fishing line** by the metre.

In English, this material is usually described as **braided fishing line** or **multifilament fishing line**.

I already had fishing line in the studio, but chose a thinner diameter for experiments where the line should remain visually discreet while still carrying mechanical tension.

The line was wound in the shop using a small **fishing-line spooling machine**. Watching that machine was unexpectedly relevant in itself: a compact device controlling tension, rotation and the orderly transfer of a flexible line from one spool to another.

It is a small piece of fishing infrastructure, but also a useful mechanical reference.

## Swivels

I also bought **Stucki Fishing Barrel Swivels with Safety Snap**, size 14, rated to 7 kg.

A swivel allows two connected parts to rotate relative to one another without transferring all of that twist into the line.

That is immediately useful for kinetic systems in which suspended elements rotate, wind or change orientation during movement.

The safety snap also introduces another useful quality: a connection can be attached and removed quickly without rebuilding the complete line assembly.

Product reference:

[Stucki Fishing — Barrel Swivel with Safety Snap](https://fischen.ch/Barrel-Swivel-with-Safety-Snap/A20970610)

## The beads I bought by mistake

I also bought a packet of **CAMO Force Beads, 8 mm, Green Pumpkin**.

I initially picked them up thinking they were small fishing weights or sinkers.

They are not.

They are **magnetic metal beads** designed for Texas and Carolina rigs. Their intended fishing function is partly acoustic: compared with glass beads, the metal produces a more discreet underwater sound, and the beads are also more resistant to impact from tungsten or steel weights.

For Artificial Emotion, the mistake may still be useful.

The beads are small, heavy relative to their size, perforated, magnetic and easy to integrate onto a line. That combination suggests other possible uses as moving masses, stops, spacers, contact elements or small components in experimental mechanisms.

Product reference:

[CAMO Force Beads](https://www.camo-tackle.de/en/CAMO-Force-Beads)

## Knots as mechanical knowledge

While I was there, I asked whether they knew a good YouTube channel or video for learning fishing knots.

Instead, they gave me a small printed booklet about knots.

The text is in German, but the diagrams are highly visual and can be followed almost as a sequence of mechanical instructions.

This is useful because knots are not decorative details in this kind of work. They determine how tension is transferred, whether a connection slips, how easily a line can be replaced, and how much bulk is introduced into a moving system.

The booklet has therefore become another small technical reference in the studio.

## Fishing as a parallel technical culture

Fishing equipment interests me because it comes from a mature material culture built around managing flexible lines under changing forces.

A fishing system may combine line, knots, swivels, beads, weights, clips, rods, reels and pulleys, each solving a very specific relationship between **movement, tension, friction, rotation and release**.

Those relationships are also central to many of the mechanisms I am building.

I do not need to reproduce fishing techniques literally. What is useful is recognising that another field has already developed a large catalogue of compact solutions for problems that kinetic sculpture repeatedly encounters.

The fishing shop therefore becomes less a source of specialised hobby equipment and more a **local library of mechanical possibilities**.

## Earlier use: Petal for Tree

This is not the first time fishing materials have entered my kinetic practice.

In **A Petal for Three**, fishing-line materials and techniques already became part of the practical vocabulary for suspending and moving elements.

That earlier project is a useful reminder that technical knowledge often travels sideways: a material developed for one activity can become completely different once it enters an artistic system.
