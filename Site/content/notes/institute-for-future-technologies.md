## How I arrived at IFT

My relationship with the **Institute for Future Technologies (IFT)** began through a week of teaching, prototyping and conversation in Paris (September, 2026).

I was invited by **Mariana Tamashiro** and **Xiao Xiao** to lead a workshop at the beginning of the academic year, as part of the warm-up programme around the Creative Technology students and its hackathon. The students were entering an advanced stage of their engineering education in which project-based experimentation, making and interdisciplinary work become central.

I arrived to teach, but the week quickly became more than a workshop.

It was a chance to spend time inside the institute, work alongside students, see how they approached unfinished problems and exchange ideas with researchers and practitioners around IFT. I also reconnected with Mariana after almost a decade, met Xiao Xiao in person, and met again with **Vivien Roussel**, whom I had first encountered in Paris through iGEM.

That combination of teaching, making and being around people willing to test strange ideas together became important to the way I started thinking about the project that would become *Artificial Emotion*.

## Sensitive Machines

The workshop was developed around **Sensitive Machines**: an invitation to imagine machines not only as functional systems, but as things that can sense, respond, hesitate, exaggerate, behave and become physically expressive.

The participants worked through rapid prototyping with simple electronics, physical interfaces and computational behaviours. Rather than beginning with polished outcomes, the emphasis was on making something tangible quickly enough that it could be observed, questioned and changed.

The title stayed with me.

What happens when a machine appears sensitive?

What kinds of movement make us read intention, hesitation, tension or vulnerability into an object?

How little does a mechanism need before we start projecting emotion onto it?

These questions were already close to concerns that had been moving through my practice, but the week at IFT gave them a shared context and a new physical urgency.

## From Sensitive Machines to Artificial Emotion

*Artificial Emotion* did not arrive at IFT as a finished project.

It emerged from being there.

During that week, conversations around behaviour, sensing, interaction and machines began to converge with my own interest in kinetic systems, bodies, textiles and expressive movement. What had started as a workshop theme began to open into a larger artistic question: whether emotion could be approached not as something represented by a machine, but as something perceived through the machine's physical behaviour.

That shift is one of the origins of *Artificial Emotion*.

The project later developed into an ongoing investigation of movement, soft mechanisms, sensing and bodily signals, but IFT remains part of its genealogy: not simply as a host institution, but as one of the places where the question became concrete enough to build around.

## Artist-in-Residence

From October 2026, I am an institutional **Artist-in-Residence at IFT**.

The residency gives *Artificial Emotion* access to workshop and laboratory infrastructure, technical resources and an environment where engineering, design, research and experimental practice overlap.

For the project, this makes IFT a place for:

- developing and testing kinetic prototypes;
- investigating mechanisms, materials and movement;
- exchanging ideas with students and researchers;
- connecting artistic questions with engineering approaches;
- documenting works in progress;
- allowing new collaborations and research directions to emerge from the process.

The residency is therefore not separate from the work. It is one of the environments through which the work is being made.

## People around this note

A large part of my relationship with IFT is also a relationship with people.

**Mariana Tamashiro** was one of the bridges that brought me into the institute. We had known each other for years but had often been in different countries, so working in the same place again after almost a decade gave the week a particular sense of continuity.

**Xiao Xiao**, director of IFT, helped create the institutional context in which this exchange could happen and in which the residency could continue.

**Vivien Roussel** is another connection between different moments of my research network in Paris, following an earlier encounter through iGEM.

Around them is a wider group of students, researchers and collaborators whose conversations, prototypes and questions form part of the environment in which the project is developing.

As these relationships become more relevant to *Artificial Emotion*, they can become Notes of their own and connect back here.

## About IFT

IFT is a transdisciplinary institute within **De Vinci Higher Education** in Paris, bringing together research, education, emerging technologies and experimental forms of making.

Its Creative Technology activities place strong emphasis on project-based learning and on turning ideas into tangible systems. The institute also operates laboratory and fabrication environments that support work across areas such as digital fabrication, artificial intelligence, biotechnology and interactive technologies.

This context is particularly relevant to *Artificial Emotion* because the project moves continuously between artistic intuition and technical construction: motors, sensors, materials, code, physical behaviour and the question of how those systems are perceived by a human body.
