## Movement as material

Kinetic Studies investigates movement as a material in itself.

The starting point is mechanical: motors, spools, pulleys, cables, guides, counterweights, supports, switches and simple forms of actuation. But the research question is not only whether a mechanism works.

It is what kind of movement it produces.

A motor can pull.

A spool can wind.

A cable can lift or release a suspended element.

A microswitch can define a limit.

A repeated cycle can become rhythm.

A pause can become hesitation.

A small mechanical action can begin to be read as gesture.

The research therefore moves continuously between engineering and perception. A movement may be technically identical while feeling completely different depending on its speed, acceleration, repetition, timing, amplitude or relation to gravity.

## From mechanism to behaviour

The research follows a simple chain:

**Mechanism → Motion → Rhythm → Behaviour → Perception**

A mechanism creates a physical possibility.

Motion gives that mechanism a trajectory.

Rhythm organises movement through time.

Repeated patterns begin to suggest behaviour.

Behaviour is finally completed in perception: by the viewer who reads tension, urgency, hesitation, insistence, collapse, anticipation or calm into the moving object.

The aim is not to imitate human emotion literally.

It is to investigate the minimum mechanical conditions under which movement starts to acquire character.

## Mau Mau — the first kinetic module

The first dedicated research object in this thread is currently being developed under the working name **Kinetic Module / Mau Mau**.

Mau Mau is a compact electromechanical module built around a stepper motor, spool, guide system and homing mechanism. Its current development combines:

- a 28BYJ-48 stepper motor
- ULN2003 motor driving
- ESP32-S3 Super Mini control
- a microswitch used for homing
- a 3D-printed spool
- a laser-cut enclosure system
- cable / filament routing
- parametric mechanical design in Rhino and Grasshopper
- electronics and PCB development in KiCad

The current files document Mau Mau primarily as an experimental mechanical and electronic platform. Its final expressive behaviour is still being developed.

That unfinished state is useful.

The module allows movement to be studied before it becomes part of a larger sculpture. Direction, travel, speed, pauses, homing, tension and repetition can be isolated and tested as individual variables.

Mau Mau is therefore not intended as a finished artwork on its own. It is the first member of a possible family of kinetic modules.

## Why modules

Artificial Emotion will likely require more than one kind of movement.

A modular system makes it possible to develop those movements incrementally.

Instead of designing a complete sculpture and then solving all of its mechanisms at once, each module can become a research object with a specific mechanical capacity.

One module might pull vertically.

Another might introduce lateral tension.

Another might rotate, vibrate, release, oscillate or work against a counterweight.

The module becomes a unit of experimentation.

This allows different questions to remain separate long enough to be understood:

- How much travel does a movement need?
- What changes when the same action is slower?
- When does repetition become mechanical and predictable?
- When does irregularity begin to feel intentional?
- What happens when several modules move together?
- When does synchronisation feel organised?
- When does desynchronisation feel alive?

## Rhythm, delay and hesitation

A central question in Kinetic Studies is how temporal qualities change the reading of movement.

Mechanical systems often optimise for consistency.

Artistic behaviour may require the opposite.

A movement can become expressive through:

- acceleration
- deceleration
- pauses
- delayed response
- incomplete movement
- repetition
- asymmetry
- variation
- oscillation
- interruption
- gradual release

A perfectly repeated cycle may read as machine-like.

A movement that slows before reaching its limit may feel cautious.

A brief reverse movement can look like correction.

A delayed response can look like hesitation.

These readings are projections made by the viewer, but the mechanical system creates the conditions for them to emerge.

## Gravity, suspension and return

Many of the current mechanisms operate through pulling rather than pushing.

This makes gravity part of the system.

A suspended object can be lifted by a motor and returned by its own weight. A counterweight can change the apparent effort of a movement. Elasticity can create delayed return. A line passing through a pulley can separate the position of the motor from the visible movement of the object.

These arrangements are technically simple, but perceptually rich.

They also allow mechanisms to remain physically distributed across the installation: motors, cables, hanging bodies and moving elements do not need to occupy the same place.

This becomes especially relevant as Artificial Emotion grows from individual tests toward larger kinetic compositions.

## Building a vocabulary of movement

Kinetic Studies is developing a vocabulary through repeated tests rather than through fixed definitions.

Examples may include:

- **steady pull → lifting / contraction**
- **slow release → yielding / collapse**
- **short repeated travel → trembling / insistence**
- **alternating direction → oscillation / uncertainty**
- **pause before reversal → hesitation**
- **multiple unsynchronised modules → distributed behaviour**
- **shared rhythm → collective movement**

These associations are not universal.

They are observations to be tested.

The same mechanical movement can be read differently depending on material, scale, sound, context and the presence of other moving elements.

## Research objects

The thread develops through physical prototypes, mechanisms and motion tests.

Some remain bench experiments.

Some become reusable kinetic modules.

Some may later be integrated into larger works.

The research record can include:

- actuator tests
- motor and spool systems
- homing and limit mechanisms
- suspension experiments
- pulley arrangements
- tension systems
- counterweight studies
- timing and rhythm tests
- firmware experiments
- movement diagrams
- failures and mechanical limitations
- video documentation of behaviour

These objects are not only engineering steps.

Each one tests a relationship between mechanism and perception.

## Connection to Artificial Emotion

Kinetic Studies provides one of the behavioural foundations of **Artificial Emotion**.

If Soft Mechanics investigates how material structure can become a moving body, Kinetic Studies asks how that body moves through time.

The thread focuses on the transition from actuation to gesture:

from a motor turning,
to a line moving,
to a suspended object responding,
to a repeated sequence becoming rhythm,
to rhythm being perceived as behaviour.

As more kinetic modules are developed, the research can expand from isolated movement to coordination, interference and collective behaviour.

The larger question remains open:

> When does movement stop being read as motion and begin to be read as intention?
